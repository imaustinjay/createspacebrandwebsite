The Collection Program

Eight weeks. Twenty seats. One brand, actually built.

Not a course about branding — a cohort that finishes with a working brand: the identity, the visuals, the offer framework, the systems, a live site and storefront, the content engine, and the community practice around it.

Be told first

The program runs in cycles, and each one is announced at least a month before it starts. Leave your email and we'll write when applications open — and not before.

One email, about this. Nothing else is added to a list.

What gets built.

Every week ships a real artifact. By week eight you don't have notes about a brand — you have one, live, and it's yours.

Weeks unlock on the cohort's shared calendar, so twenty people are on week three together. That's what makes the room worth being in.

Weeks 1–2 · Identity and visuals

Your why turned into decisions — name, voice, the one-line promise — then a working brand kit: palette, type, imagery, marks. Not a mood board.

Weeks 3–4 · The framework and the systems

What you actually sell, and the architecture between the pieces. Then the operating system behind it: the workspace, the pipeline, the asset library, the templates.

Week 5 · Websites and storefronts

The site built in Wix and the storefront in Shopify, hands-on, carrying your offer map into real products. Live by the end of the week.

Week 6 · Content formats and positioning

The niche answer, your pillars, the formats you'll actually ship — and a thirty-day plan at a pace you could hold for a year.

Weeks 7–8 · Community, and the showcase

Turning an audience into a community, engagement systems that don't burn you out — and the showcase, where you present the brand you built.

What eight weeks actually asks of you.

Two lectures a week

Live, and recorded — sixteen across the cycle, yours to keep afterwards.

A workshop every second week

Build-alongs and critiques: the kit, the systems, the formats, and the closing showcase.

Office hours, weekly

Not recorded, on purpose — so people ask the questions they'd never ask on tape.

Twenty-eight live sessions in all, plus the Studio: a shared room where work in progress gets shown before it's ready, and someone answers. Roughly six hours a week, honestly counted.
